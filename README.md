# CS255-System-Analysis-and-Design
8-2 Business requirements document, and system design document

Briefly summarize the DriverPass project. Who was the client? What type of system did they want you to design?
  The DriverPass project involved designing a system to help students pass their driving test. The client DriverPass, a company that wanted to improve access to educational resources. They needed a web-based system to provide online learning material, practice tests, training packages, driving lesson scheduling, and management features. The system also needed to support multiple user roles including customers, instructors, secretaries, IT administrators, and the owner. 
  
What did you do particularly well?
  One aspect I did particularly well on was identifying both functional and nonfunctional requirements of the system. I analyzed the client interview to gather the client's needs and expectations. I then translated those needs into system features and user requirements. I also used this information to create diagrams and models that help visualize how the system will function.    
  
If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?
  If I could choose one part of my work to revise it would be the system diagrams. While they accurately depict the system's primary functions, I believe I could include more detail and depth. I would improve them by including more steps and decision points to provide a clearer picture of how data flows through the system.  

How did you interpret the user’s needs and implement them into your system design? Why is it so important to consider the user’s needs when designing?
  I interpreted the user's needs by analyzing the client interview and identifying the goals, challenges, and expectations for the system. I implemented this in the requirements of the design by including features like online practice tests, lesson scheduling, and role-based access. Considering the user's needs is important because the system's success depends on how it solves the user's problem and meets their expectations. 

How do you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a system?
  My approach to designing software begins with gathering and analyzing requirements from the client. I focus on understanding the problem that the software is going to solve. Next, I create models like use case diagrams, activity diagrams, and class diagrams to visualize the system. In the future I plan to continue using techniques such as requirement analysis, object-oriented design, and UML modeling. These strategies help me ensure the final system meets the client's expectations.
